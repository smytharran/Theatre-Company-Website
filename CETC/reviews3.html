Productions

Linda Coakley

The Irish Times

Bringing a beloved television classic to the stage is no small task, yet this production of Fawlty Towers rises to the challenge with remarkable energy, precision, and a deep affection for the original material. From the moment the lights went up, the audience was transported straight into the chaotic world of Basil Fawlty’s seaside hotel, recreated with such attention to detail that it felt as though the performers were picking up exactly where John Cleese and company left off. At the heart of the production is a pitch-perfect performance as Basil—capturing not just the physicality of the character but the frantic, simmering tension that makes him such a comedic icon. Every flailing gesture, eye twitch, and muttered insult landed with impeccable timing. What makes the portrayal so successful is that it never feels like an imitation. Instead, it channels the spirit of Basil while allowing the actor’s own personality to shine through. Sybil, Manuel, and Polly are equally memorable, forming a quartet with undeniable chemistry. Sybil’s sharp wit and shrill bursts of exasperation drew huge laughs, while Manuel’s endearing confusion and linguistic misunderstandings consistently brought the house down. Polly served as the much-needed grounding force, just as she does in the original series, balancing the mayhem with charm and subtle humour. The production cleverly combines material from several fan-favourite episodes, weaving them into a cohesive narrative that still feels natural. Audiences were treated to snippets from “The Germans,” “Communication Problems,” and “Basil the Rat,” each moment familiar enough to spark nostalgia but adapted in a way that works beautifully on stage. The direction is tight, the pacing brisk, and the comedic escalation masterfully executed. By the time the performance reached its chaotic climax, the theatre was echoing with laughter. The set deserves special praise. The Fawlty Towers lobby and dining area were recreated with impressive accuracy, down to the tartan upholstery and vintage signage. Scene transitions were seamless, supported by clever lighting cues and energetic musical interludes that kept the comedic rhythm flowing even between scenes. What elevates this production beyond a simple homage is its understanding of what made Fawlty Towers so enduring: the razor-sharp dialogue, the escalating misunderstandings, and the sense of a world that feels just slightly off-kilter. Every scene was infused with this spirit, resulting in a show that honours the original while feeling fresh and alive in its own right. Above all, the audience engagement was remarkable. From the first chuckle to the final roar of applause, the show never once lost the crowd’s attention. It’s rare to see a comedy that generates such consistent, genuine laughter—laughter that comes not only from clever writing but from the cast’s unwavering commitment to every absurd moment. This Fawlty Towers production is a delightful, high-energy celebration of classic British humour. It brings familiar characters to life with warmth and respect, captures the joyful chaos of the original series, and delivers an evening of pure entertainment. Whether you’re a lifelong fan or new to the world of Fawlty Towers, this is a show that will leave you grinning long after you’ve left the theatre.

Darragh McGrath

The Independant

Reimagining a cultural staple like Fawlty Towers is a daring endeavour, but this stage production manages to pull it off with flair, intelligence, and an infectious sense of fun. Rather than attempting a word-for-word recreation of the classic episodes, the creative team opted for a fresh adaptation that captures the essence of the beloved sitcom while offering contemporary audiences something new. The performance shines thanks to its cast, whose dedication to character work elevates the entire show. The actor portraying Basil delivers a masterclass in physical comedy; his frantic pacing, explosive eruptions of frustration, and acrobatic attempts to maintain order in the hotel resulted in some of the biggest laughs of the night. It’s clear he understands Basil not just as a caricature, but as a man constantly at war with his own circumstances—and often himself. Sybil’s portrayal is another standout, skillfully blending sarcasm, authority, and unexpected moments of tenderness. Her chemistry with Basil is electric, producing comedic sparks in every scene. Manuel’s performance might be the most crowd-pleasing of all: his earnest attempts to please guests, despite his perpetual confusion, create moments of heartfelt hilarity. Polly serves as the grounding voice of reason, navigating the madness with grace and perfectly timed dry humour. One of the production’s strengths lies in its fluid structure. Rather than confining itself to a single storyline, it weaves together a series of comedic mishaps—an unruly guest, a misplaced money box, a suspicious health inspector, and the hotel’s ever-present financial strain. These elements blend seamlessly into a narrative that escalates with each passing minute, culminating in a brilliantly orchestrated final sequence where every misunderstanding explodes at once. The direction is confident and meticulous. Each movement, gesture, and prop interaction seems crafted for maximum comedic impact. The actors navigate the stage with athletic precision, turning even the smallest action—like a tray of drinks or a ringing telephone—into a source of laughter. Timing, always the lifeblood of farce, is handled expertly. The staging and design strike a perfect balance between nostalgia and theatrical creativity. The set is instantly recognisable yet cleverly arranged to support rapid entrances, slamming doors, and the physical chaos that defines the show. Lighting cues enhance the comedic beats, while sound effects (including the occasional surprise gag) amplify the farce without overwhelming it. What makes this production truly special is its emotional undercurrent. Amid the slapstick antics, there are subtle moments that reveal the humanity of the characters. Basil’s longing for approval, Sybil’s hidden vulnerabilities, and Manuel’s unflagging loyalty add depth to the humour, reminding audiences why Fawlty Towers remains such a treasured part of British comedy. The result is a vibrant, joyous celebration of a classic series—one that appeals not only to longtime fans but to newcomers discovering the characters for the first time. It’s a production filled with heart, hilarity, and a clear love for the source material.

Myra Shaw

RTE

If you’re searching for a theatre experience that guarantees non-stop laughter, look no further than this outstanding production of Fawlty Towers. With its perfect blend of nostalgia, slapstick, and sharply delivered wit, the show captures everything audiences adore about the original series while giving it new life onstage. From the very first scene, the cast establishes complete command over the comedic rhythm. Basil strides onto the stage with a hilarious combination of pompous confidence and frazzled anxiety. The actor’s interpretation nails the character’s contradictions—the man who wants to be respected but is perpetually undone by his own impatience, pride, and poor decisions. His performance is both familiar and wonderfully fresh, offering surprises even to fans who know the classic episodes by heart. Sybil is a force to be reckoned with, portrayed with impeccable comedic timing. Her sharp one-liners and dismissive glances at Basil earned delighted laughter throughout. Manuel, meanwhile, steals scene after scene. His expressive reactions, slapstick pratfalls, and genuine sweetness provide the kind of endearing humour that won over the entire audience. Polly remains the calm in the storm, grounding the chaos while delivering her own understated jokes with great finesse. The script cleverly brings together several iconic Fawlty Towers incidents while introducing a few original twists. The result is a narrative that feels fast-paced and unpredictable, escalating from simple misunderstandings to total pandemonium in true Fawlty fashion. Whether it’s an impossible guest demand, mistaken identity, or Basil’s ill-conceived attempts to fix a problem by making it worse, every scene builds upon the last. Production design plays a major role in the show’s success. The set is both beautifully nostalgic and highly functional, allowing for rapid pacing and well-executed physical comedy. Every door slam, prop mishap, and chaotic chase is choreographed with clear expertise, ensuring the farce remains sharp and cohesive. Costumes also add to the immersion, staying faithful to the era and instantly recognisable characters. One of the highlights of the evening is the cast’s interaction with the audience. Without breaking the fourth wall unnecessarily, the actors draw viewers into the hotel’s madness, making the entire theatre feel like part of the Fawlty Towers experience. The laughs come not just from the jokes but from the anticipation—the audience quickly learns that any simple situation might explode into disaster at any moment. The direction deserves special recognition for its balance of homage and innovation. It honours the iconic beats fans expect while embracing theatrical possibilities the TV format couldn’t offer. The pacing is brisk without ever feeling rushed, and every comedic moment is given space to land effectively. What stands out most, however, is the sheer love poured into the production. It’s evident that the cast and crew understand both the humour and the heart of Fawlty Towers. They treat the material not as a relic to imitate but as a living piece of comedy worth celebrating, and their passion is infectious. By the final curtain, the audience was buzzing with excitement, laughter still echoing through the theatre. This production is not just a tribute to a comedy classic—it’s a joyous theatrical event in its own right. Whether you're a lifelong fan or simply looking for an evening of brilliantly executed farce, this Fawlty Towers is an absolute must-see.
